MakanLah
Kuala Lumpur

What It Is

Every pick shows the post it came from.

A restaurant recommender for Malaysia, built on what Malaysians already wrote about food. Not a model's summary of it. The posts themselves, with the writer's name on them, in whatever language they were written in.

The Problem

A confident answer with nothing behind it.

Ask a chatbot where to eat in Kuala Lumpur and it will answer. It will sound certain. It will not tell you where that came from, when, or whether the place is still open, because it does not know. It is finishing a sentence.

Meanwhile the answer already exists. Malaysians write about food constantly, on RedNote and on Google Maps, in English and Malay and Chinese, often inside one sentence. Nobody had put it in front of a hungry person with the receipts attached.

The Hard Part

What it refuses to say.

Anything can sound helpful. The engineering that matters here is the refusals, because every one of them is a place where guessing would have been easier and would have read better.

Asked something no post covers
“The provided posts do not cover whether this place is halal.” Nothing to cite. She will not guess it for you.
A cited post has gone
“This post no longer opens.” The dead row stays on screen. Dropping it quietly would make the evidence look stronger than it is.
Nothing in range serves it
“Nothing within 1 km of you serves roti canai.” Then the nearest places that do, each marked with how many of its posts still open. A real restaurant nobody has written about is not the same as one you can read about, and the two are never rendered alike.
Nobody wrote down a price
Nothing at all. No band inferred from the neighbourhood or the cuisine. The same rule holds for halal, and for the same reason.

How It Holds Up

Four commitments, made in the architecture.

  • No single source is load-bearing. Not for legal cover, for uptime. Any one platform can go dark mid-sprint. Aggregating across platforms means the app degrades rather than dies.
  • Nothing is fetched while you wait. Posts are captured in the background and read from a normalised local corpus. Freshness is a background concern, never a request-path one.
  • Two workloads, two runtimes. A person waiting for a recommendation and a scraper grinding through a backlog want opposite things. They were kept apart from the first commit rather than split later.
  • Every result cites its source post. A ranked entry without a real post behind it is not a result. It is a hallucination with a rating, and the ranker drops it before it can reach a screen.

This is Kuala Lumpur, and it is a start. The pipeline points at another city unchanged; what does not transfer is the patience to read what people actually wrote.
